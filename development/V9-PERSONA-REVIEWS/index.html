    v9.0 Persona Reviews

    Four reviewers drove the real UI on the live Orlok node — not reading the code, using the product — each playing a different person:

    1. The outsider — never seen an animatronic, no docs open.
    2. The admin/builder — wires the servos, calibrates, configures the voice.
    3. The show operator — 7:40pm on Halloween, one phone, one hand, cold, dark.
    4. The fleet runner — several props plus video screens, wants a show.

    They were told to be brutal. They were. ✅ FIXED marks what shipped the same night; everything else is open, with priority.

    What all four found independently

    The same three things, from four unrelated starting points:

    1. The product asserts success it has not established. Every reviewer hit this in a different place — a green "Hardware: Connected" badge that is static HTML, a calibration panel reporting a position the servo is not at, an emergency stop rendering 0/5 ok in green, a part test saying "tested at position 50" for a part that refused to move.
    2. The safety layer was invisible to the person it protects. The best operator documentation in the repository lives in config/hardware-safety.json — why the elbow is dead, which fuse it shares, exactly how to re-enable it — and not one character of it reached any screen.
    3. The controls that matter most were the least designed. Emergency stop, sweep test, and the play/delete pair were all measurably worse than the surrounding UI.

    The five safety findings, and what happened to them

    1. The quarantine had a hole where newcomers are steered — ✅ FIXED

    The outsider selected the physically dead, quarantined elbow on the calibration page, clicked Sweep Test, and drove it to 45° and 135° — outside even its own 78–112 window — receiving success: true, clamped: false three times.

    Cause: server/calibration/router.js drives adapters directly and never passes through controlPart(), where the safety layer lives. So the entire /api/calibration/* surface went around blockAllMotion, the angle windows, and the fused-rail serialization that every other path honours.

    Fixed: every movement endpoint evaluates the safety layer first and refuses with the reason. Verified live — parts 3 and 4 refuse with their full explanations, while the head still moves and honestly reports clamped: true.

    2. Sweep Test was the most dangerous control in the product — ✅ FIXED

    One click, no confirmation, three round trips at 100% speed, taking its span from profile.bounds. For part 5 (Forearm) those bounds are an autoGenerated 0–180 placeholder, because that part's travel has never been measured. So the button drove an uncalibrated servo across its full mechanical span, repeatedly, at full speed, on the rail whose fuse has blown before and whose other servo is dead.

    Fixed by giving part 5 a deliberately narrow 95–125 guard centred on where it was found resting. Verified: 0° lands at 95, 180° lands at 125, both reporting clamped: true. That is a guard, not a calibration — widen it once someone measures the real travel.

    3. A panic stop left the scare armed — ✅ FIXED

    emergencyStop halted playback but not lurk, motion-sensing, head-tracking or idle — all autonomous triggers. The real-world sequence: child gets frightened, operator hits stop, child steps back, the motion sensor sees it and fires the scare again. Everything that can start motion on its own is now disarmed.

    4. Five rapid panic taps fired nothing — ✅ FIXED

    The stop required a 600 ms hold. The show operator simulated what a person actually does when a child starts crying — five fast jabs — and measured zero requests. Under adrenaline people tap harder and faster, not longer and steadier, so a hold is precisely the gesture stress removes. It now fires on pointerdown.

    The asymmetry settles it: an accidental stop costs a few seconds of embarrassment; a missed stop costs a frightened child, or a hand in an actuator.

    5. A refused move corrupted the position estimate — ✅ FIXED

    controlPart returns a refusal rather than throwing, and OpenLoopLinearAdapter ignored the result — so one blocked command left the system believing a quarantined actuator sitting on its endstop was at mid-travel. That is exactly the state that makes noRetractBelowMin reasoning unsafe. The adapter now raises on a refusal, and the corrupted entry was reset to positionKnown: false.

    Show-night findings

    Measured on a phone viewport, in taps and seconds. Only one of seven jobs passed: the Lurk switch (one tap, 328×44px, 14.9:1 contrast).

    Job Taps Seconds Status
    Start the show 1 ~8s ⚠️ page load dominates
    Trigger a scare 2 + long scroll 10–14s ❌
    Say something specific 3 + typing ~20s, non-deterministic ❌
    Stop everything 1 hold 7–8s cold ✅ fixed (now fires on press)
    Recover from a freeze 2–3 12–15s ❌
    Turn the volume down 4+ and a drag ~18s ✅ fixed (slider back in the bar)
    Glance: still working? scroll + read — ✅ fixed (health was display:none on phones)

    Still open, highest value first:

    • [M] A show-night mode. One screen: SCARE, SPEAK, VOLUME, STOP. No accordions, no test scenes, no Reboot button within reach. This reframes all seven jobs at once.
    • [M] Separate play from delete in the scene list. Both are 24×23px, 8px apart. Cold thumb, dark, one-handed. You will hit delete.
    • [M] A literal "say this" box and preset phrases. The only speech input sends your text to an LLM as a prompt, so making Orlok say "nice costume" in front of a child is non-deterministic — the worst possible property. The endpoint for literal text already exists.
    • [H] Wake lock. No navigator.wakeLock anywhere. The screen sleeps between every group, and each interaction restarts with unlock + probable reload.
    • [H] Don't auto-start the MJPEG stream, and back off the eight pollers. The stream permanently consumes one of the browser's six HTTP/1.1 connections; the pollers take more. It is why the phone dies at 8:30 and why a panic request could queue behind stalled ones.
    • [H] Hide test scenes from show night. The list mixes six workshop scenes with three real ones. "Full Servo Test — All 4 Servos" will try to drive two quarantined parts in front of a family.
    • [N] Haptics. You cannot hear a confirmation over a fog machine. (Partly done — the stop control now vibrates.)

    Fleet findings

    The Fleet Command Center is a good monitoring page and not a show controller. The online count, the offline cards, and the transport errors are honest — rarer than it sounds. But:

    • ✅ FIXED — fleet ops reported success on total failure. Four fan-outs hardcoded success: true, so EMERGENCY STOP with two nodes unreachable rendered 0/5 ok in green, and the superpower buttons latched on for a state nothing had accepted.
    • ✅ FIXED — "Start Loops" never looped. /queue/start had no express.json() and start() never set the mode, so the primary unattended feature played one pass per prop and went quiet. The whole unattended story was "each prop plays one scene, then stands still until someone notices."
    • ✅ FIXED — one failed step ended a prop's night. A throw escaped runSceneWithLifecycle and left running = false permanently. audio, sayThis and askAI are now non-fatal: a network hiccup should cost a line of dialogue, not the evening.
    • [M] You cannot express a multi-prop cue. Not partially — the data model has no slot. A scene step has partId but no nodeId/characterId, the schema defines none, and the executor's only off-box call is the goblin video POST. "Orlok speaks → video plays → Mina moves" is inexpressible. Adding a targetNodeId to the step schema is the one change that turns five gadgets into a show.
    • [M] There is no clock. Scene position is the emergent sum of awaited durations; wait is a bare setTimeout with no compensation, so error is strictly cumulative and different every run. Meanwhile each hardware step spawns a fresh Python interpreter — measured ~400–800 ms with ~2× jitter, an order of magnitude wider than the ~20–50 ms perceptual sync window. Tight audio/video/motion sync is not tunable from here; it needs the persistent daemon.
    • [M] Emergency stop cannot interrupt a running scene. stopRequested is only read between scenes, so a prop mid-way through a 30 s actuator extension keeps going. Safety-relevant on a rig with a known fuse problem.
    • [H] Offline nodes render fully live controls — 11 interactive controls, none disabled, on a machine that is powered off.
    • [H] The .mixed CSS class for partial success exists and is applied nowhere, so a 3-of-5 fleet renders identically to a perfect 5-of-5.
    • [H] Two goblin registries with zero IP overlap (config/animatronics.json says .160/.161; data/goblins.json says .40/.14/.106), and green status dots on cards labelled OFFLINE.

    Admin findings

    Rated 2/10 on the question "can the UI tell me whether a part is actually working?" — and the 2 points are entirely for /setup/jaw-animation, which does it properly for exactly one part and is the model everything else should copy.

    • ✅ FIXED — the UI displayed a position the servo was not at. A 45° request clamped to 60° was echoed back as Current Position: 45° with clamped: false. Worse, Set Min / Set Max capture that number, so the calibration procedure recorded a lie as ground truth.
    • ✅ FIXED — refusals read like successes. success:false alongside "Part Elbow tested at position 50", with the reason discarded one layer short of the screen.
    • ✅ FIXED — the footer's hardcoded green "Hardware: Connected" on a rig with three dead limbs, and its hardcoded '6.0.0' version fallback.
    • [M] Placeholder calibration renders as real. Nine of Orlok's parts carry an autoGenerated 0–180 profile that the safety layer explicitly refuses to trust, and the UI shows it as "Min: 0° Max: 180°". It should read "Not calibrated — showing full mechanical span."
    • [M] The enforced envelope is never shown, and where it differs from the profile the UI shows the looser number (head 0–180 vs 60–120 enforced).
    • [M] blockReason reaches no screen. A quarantined part should render its reason where the slider is, with the controls disabled. The copy is already written and is genuinely good.
    • [M] The log viewer shows nothing useful. /api/system/logs reads journalctl, but the service redirects stdout/stderr to /var/log/monsterbox.{log,err} — so ~10 MB of application log, including every safety refusal, is invisible in-app.
    • [H] There is no Parts page and no Parts nav item — parts live inside "Calibration Mgmt", which is pure guesswork, and wiring fields are on a tab called "Model/Overrides".
    • [H] Deleting a part leaves its calibration profile behind, despite a confirm dialog that promises otherwise. IDs are sequential, so the next part inherits it.
    • [H] A brand-new unwired part is indistinguishable from a working one — it auto-creates a profile on first view and renders green Move / Sweep controls.

    Outsider findings

    • The first thing a newcomer sees is a browser security warning. Self-signed cert, default button "Back to safety", and http:// gives ERR_EMPTY_RESPONSE. A meaningful fraction stop here; the rest have been trained to click through a security warning to reach this product.
    • There is a beautiful welcome page nobody is allowed to see. /first-run ("Welcome to the Crypt") is warm and clear, and is unreachable because hostname auto-select always picks a character. It also promises you can switch character from the dashboard — you cannot; the dropdown has one item.
    • Two rows of controls for the same five subsystems, 650px apart, in different visual languages — the character strip and the row under the video.
    • [M] Jargon. "Lurk" is the largest control on the page and undecodable — it means attract mode. "Parrot" means repeat-what-it-hears. "Goblins" means video screens. "E-STOP" should be spelled out. (Positions v1.5) is an internal data version leaking into a heading.
    • [M] Help opens GitHub Pages in a new tab — on a device whose entire purpose is running headless in a garage on a LAN. Ship the docs locally at /help.
    • [M] Two maps of the app disagree: the Setup dropdown has 13 items, the Setup page has 10 tiles, neither is a superset, and /live — a whole show-night dashboard — is in neither.
    • [H] The character is muted by default and nothing says so, so a newcomer's most likely first action produces silence.
    • [H] Mobile tap targets: 24×23px play/delete, 16×16px checkboxes. The audio-library action column sits entirely off-screen at 390px behind an undiscoverable sideways scroll.

    The one-line version

    Every reviewer, from a different direction, arrived at the same sentence: the system knows which parts are dead, which calibration is fake, and when it clamped a command — and it told the operator none of it while showing green ticks and precise-looking numbers. For a builder whose job is not to destroy hardware, and an operator standing next to a machine near children, a confident wrong number is worse than a blank field.
